FIG 02.1 · Sheet 01 of 05From Dec 2025
MTest Labs · software-testing firm · AI Solutions Consultant

A test design tool the testers run themselves

What
An internal tool with its own interface. It gathers full project context from the firm's documentation and a structured question flow, then generates tailored test scenarios and cases. I also added a conversational assistant so the team can query the firm's knowledge.
Why
Test design was manual and slow: a full 8-hour day, or 2 to 3 days on larger projects.
How
A staged, multi-agent orchestration, grounded in the firm's own test-case library and historical issue logs. Refined through build, test and improve cycles with the testers.
Adoption
Trained the team to run it themselves and left documentation behind.
Result
Test design cut to 1 to 3 hours of supervised draft-and-review.

FIG 03 · Method

How I work

The simplest solution is often better than the most technically sophisticated one.

  1. 01

    Spend time with the team

    Understand how things are done today, the systems used and the day-to-day problems.

    Investigate
  2. 02

    Find the repetitive step

    Look for the manual, repetitive work. Ask whether a simpler way exists before building anything.

    Identify, simplify
  3. 03

    Build with what you have

    Microsoft 365, Copilot, Power Automate and the Power Platform first. Connect systems through APIs where needed.

    Design, build
  4. 04

    Trial small

    One team, real work, before wider rollout. Agree security and support with IT.

    Trial
  5. 05

    Test with real users

    Test with the people who will use it, and refine from their feedback.

    Test, refine
  6. 06

    Keep a person accountable

    A person reviews anything consequential. Low-risk steps can run alone.

    Person reviews
  7. 07

    Document it

    Clear notes so the business can understand, support and maintain it.

    Document
  8. 08

    Hand over and help adopt

    Train people to run it themselves, and stay until it is the normal way.

    Adopt

FIG 04.4 · One page

Build, buy or simplify: a checklist for existing software

  1. Is the step needed? Remove it before automating it.
  2. Can it be simplified? Fewer handoffs, one place for the data.
  3. Does Microsoft 365 already do it? Forms, SharePoint, Power Automate, Copilot.
  4. Can two tools become one? Look for overlap to consolidate or retire.
  5. Who will support it? Agree it with the IT Manager and external IT partners.
  6. Is it secure? Check data, access and permissions first.
  7. Where does a person decide? Mark the review gate before building.
  8. Trial small, then decide. Build, buy or simplify on evidence from a real trial.

Context used, from public sources: Shanly Homes builds homes across the South East, with its head office in Beaconsfield; Sorbon Estates manages commercial property including Thames moorings, and was named Commercial Landlord of the Year at the Thames Valley Property Awards, September 2025; The Shanly Foundation cites over £33 million donated since 1969 (Foundation and Group combined). FIG 05 · Mock-up

Step through a maintenance request

Mock-up with sample data. Illustrative: how I would approach this in my first months, to be agreed with the Deputy Chairman.

Mock-up · Sample data

Step 1 of 6

  1. Request submitted. A tenant fills in a Microsoft Form: sample unit 12, leaking tap, photo attached.
  2. Logged in SharePoint. The form creates an item in a maintenance list with a reference and status "New".
  3. Routed by Power Automate. The request goes to the right property manager by site and type.
  4. Person reviews. The property manager checks it and approves the works, or asks a question.
  5. Teams notification. The team is told it is approved, and the tenant gets an email update.
  6. Power BI status. Open, approved and completed requests are visible in one view.
