001

A test design tool the testers run themselves

From Dec 2025MTest Labs · software-testing firm · AI Solutions Consultant engagement
What
An internal tool with its own interface. It gathers full project context from the firm's documentation and a structured question flow, then generates tailored test scenarios and cases. A conversational assistant lets the team query the firm's knowledge.
Why
Test design was manual and slow: a full 8-hour day, or 2 to 3 days on larger projects.
How
A staged, multi-agent orchestration, grounded in the firm's own test-case library and historical issue logs. Refined through build, test and improve cycles with the testers.
Adoption
Trained the team to run it themselves, and left documentation.
Result
Test design cut to 1 to 3 hours of supervised draft-and-review.
Result1 to 3 hours8 hours, or 2 to 3 days

  3. Send to client

After

  1. Docs and question flow
  2. Staged agents draft
  3. Person reviews: tester approves
  4. To client

03How I work

The simplest solution is often better than the most technically sophisticated one.

  1. 01

    Spend time with the team

    Understand how things are done today, the systems used and the day-to-day problems.

    Investigate
  2. 02

    Find the repetitive step

    Look for the manual, repetitive work, and ask whether a simpler way exists first.

    Identify, simplify
  3. 03

    Build with what you have

    Microsoft 365, Copilot, Power Automate and the Power Platform first. APIs to connect systems.

    Design, build
  4. 04

    Trial small

    One team, real work, before wider rollout. Agree security and support with IT.

    Trial
  5. 05

    Test with real users

    Test with the people who will use it and refine from their feedback.

    Test, refine
  6. 06

    Keep a person accountable

    A person reviews anything consequential. Low-risk steps can run alone.

    Person reviews
  7. 07

    Document it

    Clear notes so the business can understand, support and maintain it.

    Document
  8. 08

    Hand over and help adopt

    Train people to run it themselves, and stay until it is the normal way.

    Adopt

Build, buy or simplify: one-page checklist for existing software

No.QuestionWhy it matters
01Is the step needed?Remove it before automating it.
02Can it be simplified?Fewer handoffs, one place for the data.
03Does Microsoft 365 already do it?Forms, SharePoint, Power Automate, Copilot before new software.
04Can two tools become one?Look for overlap to consolidate or retire.
05Who will support it?Agree it with the IT Manager and external IT partners.
06Is it secure?Check data, access and permissions first.
07Where does a person decide?Mark the review gate before building.
08What did a small trial show?Build, buy or simplify on evidence, not on a demo.

05Mock-up: a maintenance request, step by step

Illustrative: how I would approach this in my first months, to be agreed with the Deputy Chairman.

Mock-up · Sample data6 steps
  1. Request submitted. A tenant fills in a Microsoft Form: sample unit 12, leaking tap, photo attached.
  2. Logged in SharePoint. The form creates an item in a maintenance list with a reference and status "New".
  3. Routed by Power Automate. The request goes to the right property manager by site and type.
  4. Person reviews. The property manager checks it and approves the works, or asks a question.
  5. Teams notification. The team is told it is approved, and the tenant gets an email update.
  6. Power BI status. Open, approved and completed requests are visible in one view.
